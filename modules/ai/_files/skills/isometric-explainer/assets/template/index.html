What this is

PacketPost is an isometric town where every station is one stage of a single web request. A van leaves the browser desk with nothing but a URL: it gets an address at the Name Registry, a connection at the Handshake Yard and keys at the Certificate Vault, asks the Edge Depot for the page, and — only if the depot does not already have it — drives inland to the Origin Works and the Records Hall. Then it comes home over the elevated Return Road, which is where the bytes are actually transferred.

The gauge on the van's flank is the millisecond budget it has spent so far. The crates in the bed are the bytes it is carrying. Both are read straight out of the model.

How much of it is real

Genuinely computed, live, in your browser: the whole waterfall. DNS, TCP and TLS are charged in round trips at whatever latency you dial in; TLS 1.3 costs one round trip and 1.2 costs two; the origin leg is charged at twice the edge distance; database time is queries × per-query cost; and the response transfer is the larger of the time the pipe needs for the bytes and the time TCP slow start needs to open the congestion window, starting from a ten-segment initial window and doubling each round. Time to first byte, the completion time, the round-trip count and the latency-bound / bandwidth-bound verdict all fall out of that arithmetic. It is all in js/model.js, about 120 lines.

Assumed: that the resolver sits as far away as the server; that the origin sits twice as far as the edge; that headers cost 800 bytes compressed; that one connection carries everything (a real page opens several and fetches dozens of files over them).

Indicative: parse and paint, which depends far more on the page than on the network and is the one bar here that is a guess.

Scenery: the buildings, the trees and the traffic paint. They are there so the mechanism has somewhere to happen. Treat the town as an illustration and the numbers on the panel as the lesson.

Pacing

The first time the van reaches a station it stops for long enough to read that station's write-up — between 9 and 26 seconds, depending on how much there is to say — and a progress bar under the panel text shows how much of the stop is left. Once every station has been explained there is nothing new to read, so the town runs at a watchable pace instead of a readable one. Space holds any stop indefinitely, S steps one station at a time, and the Speed slider scales everything, reading stops included. Reset (⟲) replays the slow tour; Run keeps what you have already read.

Three requests run in a row on purpose. The second and third are dramatically cheaper, and nothing about the network changed between them — DNS, the connection and the edge cache are simply warm. That gap is the single most useful thing on this page.

Controls

  • The view starts close on the van and rides with it. The ⤢ button on the left, or a double-click on the map, pulls back to the whole town.
